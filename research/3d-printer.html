RESEARCH R01 · 3D PRINTING · MECHANICAL · EMBEDDED

3D Printer Architecture

Technical study of the CoreXY motion architecture used in the scratch-built FDM printer, carried through mechanical constraints, drive arrangement, electronics and calibration considerations.

Motion architecture

Two fixed XY motors drive the gantry through coupled GT2 belt paths. Belt tension and idler alignment were treated as direct contributors to motion accuracy.

Mechanical system

Frame, linear rods, LM8UU bearings and belt-driven Z were evaluated as a low-cost fabricated motion system.

Control architecture

Raspberry Pi runs Klipper, Moonraker and Mainsail while an Arduino Uno handles the motion-control interface and DRV8825 drivers.

Calibration

Testing focused on belt tension, mechanical binding, driver current, temperature control and layer-shift faults.

Research outcome

The study was used alongside the physical printer to evaluate architecture and implementation choices before and during fabrication, then narrow debugging toward the motion, drive and calibration subsystems that affected real print behaviour.
