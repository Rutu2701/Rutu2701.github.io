SOURCE DOCUMENT · PARAFOIL DESIGN STUDY

Guided Parafoil Recovery for Targeted Landing

Web copy of the uploaded Parafoil_Design_Study.docx, prepared as the design-study/PDR addendum for CAN-7USAT.

Abstract

The study evaluates replacing the fixed hemispherical main parachute with a steerable ram-air parafoil for targeted landing. A 0.40 m², 5-cell parafoil with AR 2.7 gives a calculated 3:1 glide ratio, 7.41 m/s horizontal speed and 2.47 m/s sink rate. At 600 m deployment altitude, the theoretical no-wind glide range is 1.755 km. The recovery system is estimated at 99 g versus 226.4 g for the baseline main chute.

Requirements

Descent rate ≤3 m/s; deployment at 600 m AGL ±10 m; autonomous GPS-targeted glide; landing accuracy goal ≤50 m in the nominal no-wind case; recovery-system mass ≤226 g; mechanical fail-safe fallback; reuse existing GPS, IMU and flight computer.

Aerodynamic design

Design mass: 1.05 kg. Air density at 600 m: 1.125 kg/m³. Selected area: 0.40 m². CL=0.75, CD=0.25. Trim airspeed: 7.81 m/s. L/D=3.00. Glide angle: 18.4°. Horizontal speed: 7.41 m/s. Sink rate: 2.47 m/s. Span: 1.04 m. Mean chord: 0.38 m. Wing loading: 25.8 N/m² ≈2.62 kg/m².

Sizing trade study

0.30 m² gives 3.47 m/s sink rate and is rejected. 0.50 m² gives 1.94 m/s sink rate but increases pack volume and reduces wing loading. The 0.40 m² configuration is retained as the baseline.

Glide range

With 15 m reserved for flare, usable descent height is 585 m. Descent time is 236.8 s and theoretical no-wind range is 1,755 m.

Guidance, navigation and control

GPS latitude/longitude are converted to local east/north offsets using an equirectangular approximation. Target bearing is computed with atan2(Δx, Δy). Heading error is wrapped to ±180° and passed to a proportional differential-brake controller. The loop runs at 5 Hz on the Teensy 4.1. Within approximately 30 m of the target or below approximately 20 m AGL, control switches to symmetric flare.

Actuation

Initial brake-line load is estimated as 15% of vehicle weight: approximately 1.55 N. With a 10 mm servo horn, required torque is approximately 0.0155 N·m (0.158 kgf·cm). The existing MG90S, rated at approximately 1.8 kgf·cm at 4.8 V, provides more than 11× the estimated static requirement. Physical testing is required to validate the line-load assumption.

Mechanical integration and mass

Pilot-chute-assisted extraction replaces the dual-spring ejection mechanism. The proposed recovery system is 99.0 g, giving a 127.4 g reduction from the 226.4 g baseline. The projected vehicle mass becomes 854.6 g against a 1,050 g competition limit.

Risk and verification

Primary risks are partial inflation, line entanglement, unsafe guidance, higher touchdown horizontal speed, GPS loss, servo failure and reduced structural margin. Verification is staged through static inflation, bench brake testing, low-altitude guided drops, full-altitude testing, GPS-loss testing and flare-timing verification.

Programme recommendation

The parafoil remains a CDR design study rather than a committed PDR baseline change. The existing hemispherical main chute remains the flight-qualified fallback until repeated drop testing demonstrates reliable inflation and controlled landing.
