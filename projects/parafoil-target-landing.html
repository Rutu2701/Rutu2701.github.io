Guided Parafoil Recovery

Design study for targeted recovery of CAN-7USAT using an actively steered ram-air parafoil. The work was carried out for the college Rocketry & CanSat Club and later submitted to IN-SPACe for CDR consideration.

Problem

The baseline hemispherical main chute provides passive descent but no control over final landing position. Wind therefore determines the landing footprint during the main-descent phase.

Proposed system

A 0.40 m², 5-cell ram-air parafoil provides a controllable recovery alternative. Differential brake-line deflection provides heading control; symmetric brake input is reserved for flare.

Guidance

GPS position and course are converted to local east/north offsets and target bearing. A proportional bearing-error controller commands differential brake input at 5 Hz, with saturation and a low-altitude flare override.

Integration

The existing GPS, IMU and Teensy 4.1 flight computer are retained. Two PWM outputs are added for brake servos; the flight-state machine gains GUIDED GLIDE and FLARE states.

Aerodynamic sizing

The trim condition was sized from weight equilibrium:

V = √(2W / (ρ S CL)) = 7.81 m/s

Three planform areas were compared. The 0.30 m² option exceeded the 3 m/s descent-rate limit; 0.50 m² reduced sink rate further but increased pack volume and reduced wing loading. The 0.40 m² configuration was retained as the baseline.

Glide range

Using a 15 m altitude reserve for the final flare, the usable descent height is 585 m.

t = 585 / 2.47 = 236.8 s
Range = 585 × 3.0 = 1,755 m

This is the theoretical no-wind range for a straight, full-glide trajectory, not a guaranteed landing radius.

Guidance and control

GPS coordinates are converted to a local frame, target bearing is calculated with atan2, heading error is wrapped to ±180° and passed through a proportional controller:

e = bearingtarget − headingcurrent
u = Kp · e

Near the target or below approximately 20 m AGL, the controller switches to symmetric flare. The design prioritises the descent-rate constraint over target tracking.

Actuation and integration

Initial brake-line load was estimated conservatively as 15% of vehicle weight. A 10 mm servo horn and existing MG90S provide substantial static torque margin; physical testing remains necessary to validate actual line loads.

Risk and verification

Key risks were partial inflation, line entanglement, unsafe guidance, GPS loss and brake-servo failure. The staged verification campaign covers static inflation, bench actuation, low-altitude guided drop, full-altitude drop, GPS-loss testing and flare timing.

The study recommends retaining the hemispherical main chute as the flight-qualified fallback until repeated drop tests demonstrate reliable inflation and controlled landing.
