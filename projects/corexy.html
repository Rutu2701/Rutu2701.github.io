CoreXY 3D Printer

Scratch-built FDM printer engineered around a CoreXY motion system, a modular electronics stack and Klipper. The build combines mechanical design, motion control, embedded electronics, Linux configuration and iterative fault diagnosis.

COREXY KINEMATICSKLIPPERSTEPPER CONTROLMECHANICAL FABRICATIONCALIBRATION

Motion architecture

Two stationary NEMA 17 motors drive the XY gantry through GT2 belts. Salvaged 8 mm steel rods and LM8UU bearings form the linear motion system.

Structure

Approximately 400 × 400 × 550 mm PVC frame with plywood/MDF structural plates and mechanical brackets.

Z axis

Belt-driven Z using a continuous GT2 loop and pulley reduction instead of lead screws.

Toolhead

MK8 direct-drive extruder, 24 V ceramic heater and radial part-cooling fan.

What this project demonstrates

This is not just a printer assembly. It is an integrated engineering exercise spanning kinematics → mechanics → electronics → firmware → Linux host → calibration → diagnostics.

Recruiter signal
Built from low-cost and salvaged components while making the motion and control architecture work as one system.

CoreXY kinematics

Both XY motors remain fixed to the frame. Motion is produced by coupled motor commands:

ΔA = ΔX + ΔY

ΔB = ΔX − ΔY

This coupling means belt tension, idler alignment, motor synchronization and frame geometry directly affect print accuracy.

Control architecture

The control stack separates the Linux host from low-level motion execution. A Raspberry Pi 4B hosts Klipper, Moonraker and Mainsail; an Arduino Uno acts as the motion MCU.

Electronics & power

  • 24 V isolated power distribution for the printer electronics and hotend.
  • Three DRV8825 stepper-driver modules with manually calibrated Vref.
  • Discrete IRLZ44N MOSFET stage for hotend switching.
  • Mechanical endstops and NTC thermistor sensing.

Engineering problem solving

Calibration covered belt tension, mechanical binding, driver current and hotend temperature control. A recurring diagonal layer-shift defect was traced to the coupled CoreXY motion system. The investigation led to belt-tension balancing, idler checks and an increased driver torque margin.

This is the useful part of the build: observe → isolate the subsystem → change one variable → test again.
