PROJECT 03 · MECHANICAL · EMBEDDED · 3D PRINTING

CoreXY 3D Printer

Designed and built a scratch-built FDM 3D printer around a CoreXY motion architecture. The project combines mechanical fabrication, stepper-driven motion, embedded electronics and a Linux-based Klipper control stack, with iterative calibration used to diagnose and improve real hardware behaviour.

Motion architecture

Two stationary NEMA 17 motors drive the XY gantry through GT2 belts. Salvaged 8 mm steel rods and LM8UU bearings form the linear motion system.

Structure

Approximately 400 × 400 × 550 mm PVC frame with plywood/MDF structural plates and mechanical brackets.

Z axis

Belt-driven Z using a continuous GT2 loop and pulley reduction instead of lead screws.

Toolhead

MK8 direct-drive extruder, 24 V ceramic heater and radial part-cooling fan.

Engineering approach

kinematics → mechanics → electronics → firmware → Linux host → calibration → diagnostics

CoreXY kinematics

Both XY motors remain fixed to the frame. Motion is produced by coupled motor commands:

ΔA = ΔX + ΔY

ΔB = ΔX − ΔY

This coupling makes belt tension, idler alignment, motor synchronization and frame geometry important to print accuracy.

Engineering problem solving

Calibration covered belt tension, mechanical binding, driver current and hotend temperature control. A recurring diagonal layer-shift defect was traced to the coupled CoreXY motion system and addressed through belt-tension balancing, idler checks and increased driver torque margin.

The practical workflow was observe → isolate the subsystem → change one variable → test again.
