PROJECT 03 · 3D PRINTING · COREXY · KLIPPER

CoreXY 3D Printer

A scratch-built FDM printer designed around a CoreXY motion system, low-cost materials and a modular electronics architecture. The control stack uses a Raspberry Pi 4B as the Klipper host and an Arduino Uno as the motion MCU.

Motion system

CoreXY gantry with two stationary NEMA 17 motors, GT2 belts and salvaged 8 mm steel rods with LM8UU bearings.

Frame

Approximately 400 × 400 × 550 mm PVC structure with plywood/MDF structural plates and mechanical brackets.

Z axis

Belt-driven vertical Z mechanism using a continuous GT2 loop and pulley reduction instead of lead screws.

Toolhead

MK8 direct-drive extruder with a 24 V ceramic heater and radial part-cooling fan.

CoreXY kinematics

Both XY motors remain fixed to the frame. The axes are generated by differential motion of motors A and B:

ΔA = ΔX + ΔY

ΔB = ΔX − ΔY

A mismatch between motor movement or belt tension produces characteristic diagonal displacement, making CoreXY belt synchronization a primary calibration concern.

Control architecture

The printer uses Klipper rather than a conventional monolithic printer firmware. The Raspberry Pi runs the host software, Moonraker and Mainsail while the Arduino Uno executes the low-level scheduled step timing.

Electronics

  • 24 V isolated power distribution for motors and hotend.
  • Three DRV8825 stepper-driver modules with manually calibrated Vref.
  • Discrete IRLZ44N logic-level MOSFET stage for hotend switching.
  • Mechanical endstops and NTC thermistor input.

Calibration & diagnostics

Initial testing focused on CoreXY belt tension, mechanical binding, driver current and hotend temperature control. A recurring diagonal layer-shift defect was traced to the coupled CoreXY motion system and addressed through belt-tension balancing, idler checks and increased driver torque margin.

Current status

Mechanical and electronics architecture documented. The project is being developed incrementally, with further printer tuning and hardware changes to be documented as the build progresses.
